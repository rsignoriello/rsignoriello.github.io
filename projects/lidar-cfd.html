Ph.D. · Project for the City of Milan · 2022–26

From raw LiDAR scans to a city model ready to simulate

A workflow of Python and C++ tools, designed and run by me, that turns raw LiDAR surveys of a Milan district into a watertight 3D model of 1,259 buildings, ready to mesh and simulate.

The problem

A wind simulation of a real neighborhood needs a clean, closed 3D model of it. Getting there from a raw LiDAR survey is usually slow, manual work: millions of points to sort, buildings to redraw as closed surfaces, and a geometry that the mesher will accept.

What I built

A chain of Python and C++ tools in five stages, each one scripted and run in turn. The LiDAR point clouds from 2022 and 2024 are cleaned and every point is classified as ground, vegetation or building (point-cloud classification). The building points are then rebuilt into a watertight 3D model, the model is meshed, and the mesh goes straight into the simulation.

Result

A model of 1,259 buildings of the La Goccia district, 156 of them at high detail, built with much less manual geometry preparation. It is the geometry behind the 38.8M-cell wind simulation that matches the measured mean wind speed within about 5%.
