What my work changed

Every CFD study starts with a mesh, and building one around a complex geometry is still slow and largely manual: NASA's CFD Vision 2030 study names it one of the main bottlenecks of the whole workflow.1

During my Ph.D. at Politecnico di Milano (2022–2026) I worked on it at two levels. First the method: cutMesh, a cut-cell library that meshes any closed shape on a Cartesian grid automatically, tested on 9 benchmark geometries and a standard OpenFOAM case. Then a real application: a workflow that turns raw LiDAR scans of a Milan district into wind and thermal-comfort simulations, which match the measured mean wind speed within about 5%.

About me

From aircraft to cities, and beyond

I'm Italian and studied aerospace engineering at Politecnico di Milano, from my bachelor's to my Ph.D. in Energy and Aeronautical Sciences. Aircraft taught me the fundamentals: fluid dynamics, numerical methods and a healthy respect for validation.

During my Ph.D. I applied those tools to the ground: the wind, heat and comfort of real urban districts. That meant building the whole workflow myself, from raw data to the final simulation: classifying LiDAR point clouds into ground, trees and buildings, rebuilding a 3D model of 1,259 buildings, meshing it, and running large OpenFOAM simulations on a cluster, compared with measurements from the field. Where tools were missing, I wrote them: cutMesh, a cut-cell library that meshes any closed shape automatically.

For six months I was a visiting researcher at the Université libre de Bruxelles, where I built the point-cloud classifier now used in the Milan workflow and extended it toward deep learning by fine-tuning the RandLA-Net network on GPU. Back in Milan, I started running clusters myself.

The toolkit is not specific to cities: turbulence modeling, heat transfer, meshing of complex geometry, validation against measurements and parallel computing are exactly what energy, aerospace and process engineering rely on. That's where I want to apply it next.

Today I live in Houston and work across English, Portuguese and Italian.
